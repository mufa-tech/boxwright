Check your port tuning with a tone sweep

A built box rarely tunes exactly where the plan said. The port end, bracing and the real net volume all move it. Here is how to find the real tuning with a tone generator and your eyes, and how to correct it.

On this page

  1. Why the tuning moves
  2. The method
  3. Why not the impedance dip
  4. Cut the port to fix it
  5. Sealed boxes

Why the tuning moves

The plan assumes a net volume, an end correction for each port end, and a box with no leaks. A port that ends near a wall acts longer than it is. Bracing and a driver with a deep motor take volume. Each of these moves the tuning by a few hertz, and a few hertz near 30 Hz is a lot.

The method

  1. Connect a tone generator to the amp. Set the gain low and the bass boost off.
  2. Play a slow sweep from about 20 Hz to 60 Hz, or step through single tones 1 Hz apart.
  3. Watch the cone, or rest a fingertip on the dust cap. At the box’s tuning the cone moves least, while the port blows hardest.
  4. Note that frequency. Check it twice, sweeping up and down.

Start quiet and turn up slowly. A long tone at high level can damage the driver and your hearing.

Why not the impedance dip

Many guides find the tuning from the dip between the two impedance peaks. In our check against Hornresp, that minimum sat about 1.2 Hz below the true tuning, because of box leakage. The point where the cone nearly stops is the tuning itself, so that is what we use.

Cut the port to fix it

A longer port tunes lower, a shorter one higher. The tuning goes with 1/√Leff, so to move from the measured Fm to the target Ft: new Leff = old Leff × (Fm/Ft)², and the length to cut is the new Leff minus the end correction. Cut a little at a time and check again.

Sealed boxes

A sealed box has no tuning to find. The same slow sweep is still useful: listen for rattles, buzzing panels and air leaks around the driver and the terminal cup.
