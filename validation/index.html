How we check the numbers

Box maths is only useful if you can trust it. Here is everything the engine is tested against, the tolerances we hold it to, and why another program can still give you a different number.

In short

  • One engine, written once, with every formula sourced. The app and this site’s calculators use it; the site’s JavaScript port is tested against the engine on 963 inputs to 1e-9.
  • 338 reference vectors run on every build: hand-worked values, edge cases, and 79 values published by manufacturers and in papers. Every one must pass within its own tolerance.
  • A separate Python reference agrees with the engine on more than 4,000 values to 1e-9. 26 deliberate bugs (a sign, a constant) were each caught by the tests.
  • 7 real designs compared with Hornresp 60.60.

Against Hornresp

Hornresp is a free simulator many builders treat as the reference. We entered the same drivers (from their datasheet Fs, Qes, Qms, Vas, Sd and Re) and the same boxes in both, at 2.83 V, half space, 1 m, QL 7.

  • Ported boxes read up to 0.6 dB lower far below tuning (at 15 Hz, where output is already about 25 dB down) and within 0.3 dB in the useful band. Hornresp treats the port as its own sound source; our model, like Small’s, sums cone and port at one point.

What we promise

  • Box formulas (Qtc, Fc, F3, port length, volumes, wiring): exact to the formula, 1e-9.
  • Against Hornresp: sealed within 0.1 dB, ported within 0.7 dB from 15 to 100 Hz, tuning within 1 %.
  • Against datasheets: within the sheet’s own rounding. When a sheet disagrees with itself, the app tells you instead of picking a side (see the datasheet check).
  • Estimates are labelled: slot port end corrections, cabin gain and the port-noise limits.

Why WinISD or another calculator shows a different number

We have not compared against WinISD: there is no official download any more to test with. When two programs disagree, it is almost always one of these:

  • Net or gross volume. Some calculators take the inside size of the box. Boxwright wants net: the driver, port and bracing out.
  • Port end correction. Programs use anything from 0.6 to 0.85 × radius per end, or let you choose. One end flanged or free moves the tuning by a few percent.
  • Box losses (QL). We use QL 7 for ported boxes, Small’s typical value. A lossless model gives a slightly different F3 and a sharper response.
  • Air constants. We use 343 m/s and 1.204 kg/m³ (20 °C). Small used 345 m/s and 1.18 kg/m³.
  • Which F3. −3 dB from the flat line, or −3 dB below the peak. We show both and say which.
  • The driver numbers. Two programs with two different datasheet versions will disagree. Check the sheet first with the datasheet check.
